story

I design for people who've been let down before.

In markets where people have been burned before, a product rarely gets a second try. They tell their group chat and move on. That single fact has shaped almost every design decision I've made in four years.

I spend more time than most designers on the moments where someone decides whether to believe you — the first payment screen, the empty state before anyone has reviewed you, the error message when a transfer doesn't land. Those screens rarely make it into portfolios and they're usually the ones that decide whether a product survives.

I came to design sideways. My degree is in educational management, which turned out to be less of a detour than it sounds: both jobs are mostly about working out what someone already believes before you try to change it. I took a product design certificate at cWw Tech Africa in 2023 and started as an intern at a fintech company, designing dashboards for people who were nervous about seeing their own money on a screen.

Since then I've worked across fintech, e-commerce, service marketplaces and agency client work — freelance, contract and embedded. I'm comfortable being the only designer in the room, and comfortable building the system that lets four designers work like one.

What I'm looking for

A product team where design is in the room when the problem is chosen, not handed the problem after it's been decided. Consumer or prosumer, ideally something where trust or money is at stake — that's where I do my best work and where I'm most useful on day one.

I work remotely, from Lagos, and I've been doing it since 2023. I'm not new to async, and I'm not new to being the only person in my timezone on a call.

How I work

four habits

research

Talk to eight people

Usability testing run small and often. Eight conversations beat a survey of four hundred for finding out why someone stopped.

structure

Argue with a flow, not a screen

Information architecture first. Most interface problems are structure problems wearing a UI costume.

craft

Prototype at the fidelity of the question

Interactive prototypes when the question is “does this feel right”. Greyboxes when it's “does this make sense”.

handoff

Design isn't done until it's built

Tokens, states, edge cases, and the awkward empty screens nobody specs.
