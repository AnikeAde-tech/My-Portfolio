the ledger

Decisions I'd defend.

Every portfolio lists outcomes. Almost none list what they cost. These are calls I made, the trade-off I accepted, and whether I'd make the same one again.

anywork · onboarding

Verify identity before the first job, not after.

what it costA longer signup and a measurable share of users who never finished it. We took the smaller, slower funnel because a marketplace that loses trust once doesn't get it back.

help app africa · redesign

Fix the three journeys that carried the traffic. Leave the rest ugly.

what it costA visibly inconsistent product for two quarters, and an awkward conversation about why the marketing site still looked old. A full redesign would have landed in month nine instead of month two.

minisquare mart · checkout

Show the total, including delivery, before the address form.

what it costAbandonment moved earlier in the funnel and looked worse on the top-of-flow chart. Completion still rose, because everyone who continued had already accepted the price.

nerdbug · design system

Ship twelve components properly instead of forty roughly.

what it costDesigners kept working off-system for months on anything we hadn't covered. But the twelve that existed were trusted — and trust is what makes a system get adopted at all.

forra finance · dashboard

Show the pending balance and the available balance as two separate numbers.

what it costA denser screen and a support burden explaining the difference. Worth it — a single blended number is a promise the product can't always keep, and one broken promise about money ends the relationship.

nextvibe · mobile

Design for the 4-inch screen first and let the desktop layout inherit.

what it costThe desktop experience was under-used real estate for a while, and stakeholders reviewing on laptops saw the weaker version first. But that's not where the users were.

Why this page exists

I've sat in interviews where every question was a variation of “walk me through your process.” The honest answer is that process is the easy part — it's written down, it's teachable, and everyone claims the same one.

What's harder to fake is judgement under constraint: knowing which corner to cut when the deadline is real, which stakeholder to disappoint, and which metric will look worse before it looks better. That's what this page is for.

If you want to interrogate any of these, I'd enjoy that conversation more than a portfolio walkthrough.
